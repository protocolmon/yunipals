## Shared-indexer migration

The migration implementation registers all four portal contracts in the existing Ponder source manifest, journals their canonical events and full-receipt candidates, and maintains rollback-managed current and historical projections. Confirmed SQL views feed the existing API under one validated read snapshot. Private spells, jobs, AI results, signatures and publication records stay in the private database. The grading consumer atomically enqueues confirmed requests and advances its durable position; release replay preserves existing job output.

`PORTALS_INDEXER_MODE=unified` requires an explicit database, release, read schema and competition binding. It never falls back to a standalone scanner. The legacy mode is temporary rehearsal/rollback support and its ledger entrypoint refuses to run in unified mode. See the [migration plan](unified-indexer/portals-migration-plan.md) and [operations procedure](../ops/portals/README.md) for fresh views, grants, same-boundary parity, candidate qualification and coordinated consumer cutover.

Validation completed locally: 110 portal tests, 47 retained shared-source tests, both application type checks, the canonical frontend build, native Ponder/PostgreSQL rollback and restricted-reader tests, and the browser/contracts flow including 111 entries. PostgreSQL testing also verifies atomic cursor recovery, saved AI output on release replay, and detection of an intentionally corrupted balance. These results do **not** establish production replay, cutover or completion of the rollback observation window; record those gates separately in private operator evidence.

## Player and owner behavior

- Portal NFTs use the existing TCG presentation, one shared approved artwork per kind, lifetime ranges of 1,000 IDs and immutable per-NFT gates. Admins commit recipient lists, receive Chainlink VRF randomness and deliver in chunks. Repeated recipient addresses are preserved.
- One global portal accepts cards for exactly 660 seconds. Permissionless VRF request, fulfillment and activation are separate stages. Indexing or AI delays never extend the window. Retirement closes admission immediately; the last live portal can repeat.
- Onchain eligibility uses immutable card traits and family sequence. `originalOnly` overrides sequence only. Rainbow's versioned effect recolors the creature while retaining its original base color. Players submit a signed private spell against the latest completed raw artwork.
- Initial Rainbow pricing is 0.99 USDC: 0.245 to the AI wallet, 0.245 credited to the exact portal used, and 0.50 deposited into the competition pool. Versioned admin pricing changes future AI/portal fees. The current portal owner can burn that NFT to claim its accrued balance; transfers carry the balance.
- Grading records Creativity, Harmony, Craft and Impact on a 1–100 scale, with per-kind rubrics, evidence and conservative copying penalties. A card can transfer while grading; accepted work has no timeout/refund and regrading is unlimited. Only one request per card is pending at a time.
- Admission-order challenges contain 111 paid submissions across independent 660-second portal windows and different portals/kinds. Changing portals never resets the batch. Final scores rank the top three, ties favor earlier entries, and submitting wallets receive 50/30/20 payouts. Any wallet can settle or retry a deferred prize; the contract determines payees and amounts. Prize reserves remain separate from operator funds.
- After admission #111, entry pauses until all grades complete and the three winning spells produce the next immutable theme. Individual results publish as they finish. Theme interpretation affects Harmony; shared theme ideas are exempt from copying penalties. Ready prizes remain settleable during this pause.

## Completed application and runtime work

The existing TCG collection, collector and token-detail artwork uses the canonical graded metadata once ready, while ungraded cards retain their original artwork and foil layers. Token details link to preselected spell entry and grading history. The app includes active portal entry, card history and slab downloads, owner inventory/detail/claim flows, competition standings and direct settlement, and role-controlled kind/mint/pricing administration. Exact transaction intent and identity survive navigation and reload, scoped to chain, deployment and wallet. Success waits for a canonical receipt **and matching applied portal projections**. The shared-indexer migration binds these projections to the registered global release, immutable read identity and confirmed observation. A bare global head is insufficient evidence. Version 1 remains compatible during handover; version 2 carries the shared identity.

Indexed evidence covers admission/competition entries, completion/publication, fee credits and burns, observed transfers, randomness/round activation, mint commitments and delivery chunks, competition payouts, themes and admin changes. Delays retain the submitted hash and show a status retry; replacement/cancellation and reorgs invalidate obsolete evidence. USDC approval uses a successful receipt plus a fresh allowance read. Live ownership, round expiry, input version, fee version and theme are rechecked before payment.

The worker signs into a durable transaction journal before broadcasting. A PostgreSQL signer lock coordinates grades, themes and deferred result notifications. Recovery reconciles the same nonce, intent and signed bytes; provider authorization errors do not imply absence. Compatible primary/fallback RPCs are checked separately by a read-only preflight. Unexplained nonce consumption stops automatic sends for investigation.

Dedicated portal schema selection and separate migration/API/worker/ledger grants avoid access to unrelated application data. Worker and ledger enablement are explicit. Generic service templates document startup, graceful shutdown, immutable releases, runtime roles, backup/restore and rollback. Public health reports canonical ledger/worker progress, queue age/retries, publication delay, reserve liabilities, challenge phase and optional signer balance without private spells or credentials.

New admissions require a short-lived EIP-712 stored-spell acknowledgement as an additional function argument. A dedicated API signer issues it only after committing and re-reading authenticated input and checking a fresh quote. The contract verifies wallet/admission/chain/proxy/competition bindings, expiry and the current intake role before payment. Old selectors cannot bypass the check; existing paid requests remain completable. Result/URI validation is kept in an immutable stateless helper without changing proxy storage. Generated and cached themes receive deterministic link/markup/account-instruction checks before signing; repeated unsafe output is held for review.

Confirmed card metadata uses a deterministic, content-addressed PNG slab with bundled fonts and a renderer manifest binding the raw artwork, card/version/kind and four final scores. The raw edited image remains the next AI input. History, spells, reports, display images and theme evidence require canonical indexed publication. Older publications are requeued only for display reconstruction, preserving their original copying-cutoff timestamps and saved AI results. Burned-card history remains available independently of live ERC721 metadata.

## Reproducible verification

The portal CI workflow uses pinned Foundry/Anvil and browser tooling, a disposable PostgreSQL service, deterministic AI/VRF fixtures and real local transaction receipts. No paid provider or public-chain sends occur in default checks.

- Release-profile contracts: 164 tests passed, nine opt-in checks skipped; storage layout, runtime bytecode size and generated ABI consistency passed. The current main-branch mint engine's payment-adapter integration is preserved. Its 24,573-byte runtime leaves only three bytes below EIP-170; the receipt-enabled grading implementation is 24,446 bytes. Any later Solidity change requires fresh size validation.
- The existing ETH payment launch package remains byte-for-byte unchanged. Its CI check compiles the pinned reviewed source, while the current engine and payment regressions are tested separately; a later portal-aware engine does not silently replace a reviewed deployment package.
- Portal backend/recovery/evidence/slab tests: 90 passed. Coverage includes stored-spell receipts, fresh/cached theme validation, RPC access failures, durable signed attempts, lease fencing, exact receipt/event/projection identity, reload/replacement/reorg recovery, policy compatibility and deterministic publication.

- The local browser runner covers admission/payment with delayed indexing, reload recovery, funded and zero-value burn/claim, grading after portal retirement/card transfer, canonical PNG metadata, a complete 111-entry challenge, exact payouts and entry #112 after durable winner-theme publication. Additional admin coverage includes a 35-NFT repeated-recipient list delivered in two chunks, reload restoration, future pricing and staged VRF activation.

## External review and activation

The supplied source/state audit identified single-key governance, unavailable spell submissions, activation timing, historical engine verification, restricted USDC recipients, theme text and configuration/storage hazards. Single-key governance was explicitly accepted for now. Intake acknowledgement and theme validation are implemented for retest; portal timing remains intentionally independent of competition membership. Other findings remain subject to their recorded dispositions and follow-up checks. Preserve the frozen audit archive and review the updated source delta before public activation; green CI is not audit closure. Contract upgrade authority and the current AI grader/theme publisher remain trust dependencies; permissionless settlement does not decentralize scoring.

The application PR does not activate services, send new mainnet transactions or perform the airdrop. Operators must reconcile existing setup/verification receipts, configure a compatible RPC and dedicated database/artifact access, catch up the ledger, validate retained policies and worker roles, and complete audit review. Remaining controlled live acceptance is on mainnet, following the user's selected launch path.

The airdrop rule is one new Rainbow Portal per eligible live OG Rainbow or Ghost Portal NFT, including verified staking beneficiaries. Refresh ownership at execution, preserve repeated wallet entries, resolve classification exceptions, and verify each actual delivery. Provisional recipient counts are not an execution snapshot. Keep recipient lists, credentials and execution receipts outside Git.
